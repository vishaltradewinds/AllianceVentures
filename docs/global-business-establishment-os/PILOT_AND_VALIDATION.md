# Pilot and Validation Plan

## Objective

Prove one bounded Indian-outbound business establishment case end to end, with customer consent and qualified local professional participation. Do not begin by claiming global coverage.

## Pilot selection

Select the first destination only after:
- a real prospective customer and specific business activity are identified;
- the customer can provide sufficient business requirements and authorised decision-making;
- the proposed activity is lawful to assess and there is a feasible route to qualified local review;
- official source material is accessible and the pilot scope can be bounded;
- data handling, confidentiality, and engagement terms are approved.

Do not preselect a country or industry simply because public data is convenient.

## Required test cases

1. A supported route with sufficient evidence can reach review and approval stages.
2. A prohibited foreign-ownership or sector route is blocked.
3. An unresolved legal question remains BLOCKED/PENDING_REVIEW despite strong commercial scores.
4. Stale or superseded legal sources invalidate the affected assessment pending reassessment.
5. Contradictory sources are surfaced to a reviewer.
6. A property listing without title/lease verification cannot be described as verified property rights.
7. A potential incentive is not represented as awarded or guaranteed.
8. An application is not represented as approved before actual authority evidence exists.
9. The customer can choose an export/distributor/contract-manufacturing alternative or no-go.
10. Tenant isolation prevents one customer from accessing another customer's confidential documents.
11. Every material recommendation can be traced to source evidence, assumptions, model version and reviewer.
12. Changes to legal rules trigger impact analysis for affected open cases.
13. Failed external-provider or background-job operations are recoverable and leave an auditable state.
14. Data export, retention and deletion behaviours conform to the approved policy.

## Acceptance evidence

- Approved pilot scope and customer mandate
- Versioned business requirements
- Jurisdiction module coverage statement and exclusions
- Evidence/source manifest with retrieval and review dates
- Legal and professional review records
- Reproducible feasibility model and sensitivity analysis
- Gate-by-gate pass/block report
- Security and access-control test results
- End-to-end workflow evidence
- Customer acceptance and lessons learned
- Known gaps, corrective actions, owners and deadlines

## Measures

Measure completeness of traceable material claims, critical blocker detection, legal-review turnaround, time and cost estimate variance, task completion, false-pass rate, stale-source detection, security-test results, and customer acceptance. Any false pass of a mandatory legal restriction is a release-blocking defect.

## Release policy

- Documentation-only foundation: no product-readiness claim.
- Prototype: synthetic/test data only unless approved controls exist.
- Pilot: named scope, approved participants, human supervision, and jurisdiction-specific review.
- Production: independent release review, security/privacy assessment, operational monitoring, support and incident processes, recovery tests, and documented acceptance.

A successful pilot certifies only the tested use case and bounded jurisdiction module. Additional countries, industries, and workflows require their own evidence and validation.
